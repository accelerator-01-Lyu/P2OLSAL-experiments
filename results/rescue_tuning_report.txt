## CRITICAL FINDING: Robust Gain Degeneracy

Diagnostic revealed that the expected entropy gain g_mean is NEGATIVE
(mean ~ -0.06) on all three datasets. The robust gain w_rob = max(0, DV-dual)
is therefore ALL ZERO for kappa >= 0.05. The default config (kappa=1.0)
runs with completely inactive robust gain — P2 degenerates to a pure
redundancy/diversity sampler. This explains why the original tuning grid
(kappa 0.5-10.0) produced identical results across all configs.

Corrected tuning extends kappa to [0, 1e-4, 1e-3, 1e-2, 0.1, 1.0] where
the robust gain is actually active (nonzero for 5-108 points depending on dataset).

  RECOMMENDATIONS
================================================================================

1. ROBUST GAIN DEGENERACY (root cause):
   The default kappa=1.0 is ~1000x too large. The KL ball constraint
   is completely inactive, making the "robust" component of P2-OLSAL
   non-functional. Consider either:
   (a) Setting default kappa=0.001 (active but sparse robust gain)
   (b) Fixing the posterior predictive so g_mean is positive (the root
       cause of negative g_mean is the uniform mixture eps_q=0.1 combined
       with hardcoded learning rate 0.5 in _sample_gains)

2. REDUNDANCY PENALTY (lam):
   lam=0.0 (disabling redundancy) is optimal on segment (0.780) and
   glass (0.469). The default lam=0.1 hurts performance. The redundancy
   penalty pushes away boundary samples that are actually informative.
   Recommend setting default lam=0.0 or much smaller (0.001).

3. DATASET-SPECIFIC CONFIGS:
   segment: kappa=0.1, lam=0.0 -> 0.7799
   glass:   kappa=0.001, lam=0.0 -> 0.4687
   ecoli(n=0.3): kappa=0.01, lam=1.0 -> 0.6506

4. LIMITATIONS (for manuscript Section 8.5):
   - Segment: Even with optimal tuning, P2 reaches 0.780 vs Random 0.787.
     On this dataset, random sampling is inherently strong due to balanced
     class distribution and well-separated clusters. Active learning offers
     no advantage at 10% budget.
   - Glass: P2 reaches 0.469 vs CoreSet 0.479. With only 214 samples,
     geometric coverage methods (CoreSet) have a natural advantage. The
     robust framework needs more samples to realize its statistical guarantees.
